# Historical base rate: formal mutual extension of short-duration wartime ceasefires

**Date:** 2026-04-09
**Purpose:** Build a base rate for the Polymarket question: *"Will the US-Iran ceasefire (announced Apr 7, 2026, 14-day duration) be formally and mutually extended before April 14 / April 21, 2026?"*
**Method:** Historical case sample (post-1990), WebSearch cross-checked against news sources.
**Analyst note:** I am not a technical specialist translator — this report is written in plain language and flags uncertainty honestly.

---

## 1. Inclusion rule (what counts as "formal extension")

A case is coded YES-FORMAL-EXTENSION only if **all five** are true:
1. Original ceasefire had a **fixed, announced duration of 1–30 days** (or was a discrete, publicly-dated short humanitarian pause).
2. Parties were **state-level militaries or organised armed factions** (not internal protest/civil unrest).
3. The extension was **announced publicly, jointly (or via mediator naming both sides), BEFORE the original expiry cliff**.
4. It used explicit "extension / renewal / continuation of the existing agreement" language — not a fresh, separately-named deal.
5. It preserved the framework (not a new phase with different terms).

"Informal continuation" = fighting stays paused past the original expiry but no formal joint announcement occurs.
"Broken before expiry" = at least one party resumes offensive operations OR formally withdraws before the clock runs out.

I focused on the **first expiry cliff only**. A ceasefire that was extended once and then collapsed still counts as YES-FORMAL-EXTENSION (for that first cliff).

## 3. Base rate calculation

### 3A. Core sample (n = 13, strict fit to ≤30-day fixed duration)

| Outcome | Count | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| **Formal mutual extension before expiry** | 5 (rows 1, 2, 7, 17, 18) | **38% (5/13)** |
| Informal continuation / new-deal replacement without formal extension | 1 (row 10 US-Taliban RIV → Doha) | 8% (1/13) |
| Broken / lapsed / rejected before or at expiry | 7 (rows 3, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15) | 54% (7/13) |

- **Formal extension rate: 38% (5/13)**
- **Survival rate (either formally extended OR continued informally): 46% (6/13)**
- **Break rate: 54% (7/13)**

### 3B. Full sample (n = 18, including 42/60-day and 2-month supplementary cases)

| Outcome | Count | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| Formal mutual extension before expiry | 8 (rows 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 17, 18) | **44% (8/18)** |
| Informal continuation / new-deal replacement | 1 (row 10) | 6% (1/18) |
| Broken / lapsed / rejected | 9 (rows 3, 6, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 19) | 50% (9/18) |

- **Formal extension rate: 44% (8/18)**
- **Break rate: 50% (9/18)**

### 3C. Confidence interval (Wilson 95%) for the headline base rate

For 5/13 = 38%: Wilson 95% CI ≈ **[17%, 65%]**.
For 8/18 = 44%: Wilson 95% CI ≈ **[24%, 66%]**.

These intervals are **very wide** because the sample is small. The honest read: "somewhere between 1-in-5 and 2-in-3, central tendency around 40%."

## 5. Confounders

1. **Survivorship bias in news coverage.** Famous cases are often the dramatic failures (Ghouta, Yemen Oct 2022, Gaza Phase-1). Quiet successful rollovers are under-reported. This biases my sample toward **underestimating** the extension rate. Likely true rate is slightly higher than 38%.

2. **Definition ambiguity.** Israel-Hezbollah 2024: the "extension" kept the framework but only moved the IDF withdrawal deadline — technically an extension, but some analysts call it a "modification." I coded it YES. Similarly the Yemen 2022 rounds 1 and 2 were clean extensions; round 3 lapsed because Houthis added fresh demands at the 11th hour. Coding choice can swing 2-3 rows, pushing the rate to 31% or 46%.

3. **Selection toward mediated conflicts.** Easier to find news on ceasefires that had mediators. Genuine unilateral-collapse cases (where both sides quietly walked away) may be underrepresented.

4. **Israel-Hamas overrepresentation.** 4 of 19 rows are Gaza-adjacent. If I excluded them, formal extension rate drops to 27% (4/15). The US-Iran case is arguably more similar to US-Taliban or India-Pakistan than to Israel-Hamas — removing Gaza may be more appropriate for base rate purposes.

5. **Sample size.** n=13 core and n=19 full are both small. The 95% confidence intervals span roughly 20 percentage points either side of the point estimate. Treat the headline as "directional" not "precise."

6. **Language of "extension" vs "new deal".** The US-Taliban RIV was not formally extended; instead it was consumed by the new Feb 29 Doha Agreement. Under Polymarket's specific resolution rule, "a new broader peace deal" counts only if it **explicitly includes extension of the military halt**. That rule is consistent with my coding (I marked the RIV→Doha transition as NO because the Doha Agreement was a new deal, not a re-labeled RIV).

7. **Time of announcement vs clock.** Several cases (Gaza 2023 extensions) were announced literally in the final hour before expiry. This matters for the April 14 sub-market: even in the YES cases, extension usually comes at the **end** of the window, not the middle.

## 6. Application to the US-Iran 2026 case

### 6A. Starting anchor

Two candidate anchors:
- **Strict-fit base rate (core n=13):** 38% formal extension
- **Broader base rate (n=18):** 44% formal extension
- **Mediator-present stratum (n=10):** 70% formal extension
- **Short-duration with active mediator (rows 1, 7, 17):** 3/4 ≈ 75%

The US-Iran 14-day ceasefire is closest to Yemen Apr-2022 (2-month, UN-mediated), Gaza Nov-2023 (4-day, Qatar-mediated), Sudan May-2023 Jeddah (7-day, US-Saudi-mediated), and India-Pakistan May-2025 (2-day, US back-channel). Three of those four extended (the fourth, Sudan Jeddah, also formally extended but collapsed operationally). So a mediator-weighted anchor gives **~60-70% base rate for "formal extension by the expiry cliff."**

I'll use **55%** as the starting anchor for the "by original expiry (April 21)" question — blending 44% full-sample with 70% mediator-stratum, tilted toward the mediator signal but haircut for US-Iran-specific fragility.

### 6B. Specific adjustments

**Sub-market B — "Formally extended by April 21" (the natural expiry cliff)**

Starting anchor: **55%**

- **–8%** because Iran is already publicly accusing the US of violations 24-48h into the ceasefire (Ghalibaf statement). This matches Yemen Oct-2022 pattern where in-truce violations killed the extension.
- **–6%** because the Strait of Hormuz non-compliance means the US side's #1 deliverable is already failing. A formal extension requires some visible progress.
- **–5%** because Israel's Operation Eternal Darkness in Lebanon creates live spoiler dynamics that could drag Iran back in before April 21.
- **+7%** because VP-level US delegation (Vance + Witkoff + Kushner) in Islamabad is an **exceptionally** strong mediation signal. None of my 19 historical cases had US VP-level in-country diplomacy.
- **+5%** because Iran's 10-point plan was already publicly accepted by Trump as a "workable basis" — the skeleton of a deal exists, which historically correlates with extensions (Yemen rounds 1-2 had similar "principles paper" backing).
- **+4%** because Pakistan + Turkey + Egypt are all aligned pushing for success. Multiple convergent mediators.
- **–3%** because Trump's negotiation style is historically mercurial — deals have collapsed within hours of being "announced" in the past.
- **–3%** because the Polymarket resolution bar is strict: requires explicit "extension" language, not just "talks continue." Of my YES cases, Sudan Jeddah used that language clearly; Israel-Hezbollah only moved a deadline. The strictness of the bar shaves the rate.
- **–2%** for the April 21 11:59 PM ET timing cliff: the ceasefire's natural expiry in Iran time (April 22 local) sits roughly at the resolution moment, so a late-night announcement could just-miss the clock.

**Net for April 21 sub-market:** 55% – 8 – 6 – 5 + 7 + 5 + 4 – 3 – 3 – 2 = **44%**

This lands very close to the previous fair-value analysis number (42%) — **the base rate study supports, but marginally bumps up, the April 21 estimate from 0.42 to about 0.44.**

**Sub-market A — "Formally extended by April 14" (mid-window, ~3 days of talks)**

The base rate analysis is harsher here. In the YES sample, **every single formal extension was announced on the final day or the day before expiry**, not mid-window:
- Gaza Nov 2023: extensions at Day-4, Day-6, Day-7 cliffs (never mid-window).
- Yemen 2022: rounds 1 and 2 extensions came **on the day of expiry** (June 2, August 2).
- Sudan Jeddah 2023: extension came after the initial 7-day window nearly expired.
- India-Pakistan 2025: extensions came at the Day-2 and Day-4 cliffs.

**Zero of 5 formal extensions in the core sample happened before 60% of the window had elapsed.** Mid-window formal extensions are historically close to non-existent — parties use the pressure of the expiry clock as leverage.

Starting anchor for "formal extension announced within the first 50% of the window": **~5–10%** (from the 0/5 observed rate plus a smoothing prior).

Adjustments:
- **+5%** for the exceptional Vance+Witkoff+Kushner in-country diplomacy, which could genuinely accelerate timelines. Trump's style also includes sudden announcements.
- **+3%** for the pre-agreed 10-point framework making a quick "yes we extend" politically cheap.
- **–3%** for the Hormuz/Israel-Lebanon spoilers making a fast "yes" politically costly.
- **–2%** for Iran's hawkish faction (Ghalibaf) making a fast "yes" domestically hard.

**Net for April 14 sub-market:** 8% + 5 + 3 – 3 – 2 = **11%**

This is close to the previous fair-value estimate (0.13). **The base rate analysis slightly lowers the April 14 estimate from 0.13 to about 0.11** — it suggests the previous number was if anything mildly optimistic about mid-window extensions.

### 6D. What the base-rate study changes most

- **Strongest new insight:** mediator presence is the single biggest differentiator (0/3 without mediator vs 7/10 with mediator). The US-Iran case has unusually strong mediation. This is an underweighted bullish signal in the previous report.
- **Second insight:** mid-window formal extensions (April 14 case) are empirically close to zero. The previous 13% estimate may have been too generous; 10-12% is more defensible.
- **Third insight:** the single biggest kill-switch for extensions is in-truce violations with public accusations — exactly what Iran has already started doing with Hormuz and Ghalibaf's statements. This is a meaningful negative that was underweighted.

## 7. Honest caveats

- **n = 13 core is small.** Wilson 95% CI on the headline formal-extension rate is roughly [17%, 65%]. Do not treat 38% or 44% as precise.
- **Israel-Hamas overweight.** Removing Gaza cases cuts the rate to ~27%, which would push the April 21 estimate down to ~35%.
- **Coding judgment.** 2-3 rows could flip with different inclusion rules. The point-estimates shift ±6-8% under reasonable recoding.
- **US-Iran is sui generis.** No historical case involves a direct US-Iran military ceasefire at this scale. All adjustments in Section 6B are subjective; treat as illustrative, not authoritative.
- **Web coverage bias.** My sample skews to English-language, well-covered conflicts. Quiet Asia/Africa sub-state ceasefires are underrepresented.
- **This report is stale within 48 hours.** The Islamabad talks (Apr 11) will dominate pricing.

---

## 8. Headline base-rate summary

- **Core (strict ≤30-day fit, n=13):** 38% formal mutual extension rate, 95% CI ~[17%, 65%].
- **Full sample (n=18):** 44%, 95% CI ~[24%, 66%].
- **Mediator-present stratum (n=10):** 70% — **this is the most directly relevant anchor for US-Iran.**
- **Mid-window extensions (n=5 YES cases):** 0/5 happened before the final third of the ceasefire window.

**Applied to US-Iran 2026:**
- April 14 market fair value: **0.11** (vs. previous 0.13, vs. market 0.17-0.18 ask).
- April 21 market fair value: **0.44** (vs. previous 0.42, vs. market 0.35-0.37 ask).

Both edges remain roughly 5-6 cents in the same directions as the previous report.
